[SLIDE 10 — overall F1 comparison]

First, the overall trend is that mean F1 decreases as compression increases.

The uncompressed baseline scores about zero point four three.

Truncation retains the most accuracy, followed by the enhanced variant and then our original LLMLingua implementation.

These are average results; individual questions can still improve.

[CHANGE TO SLIDE 11 — paired F1 differences and Exact Match]

Here, we compare LLMLingua directly with truncation.

F1 is about zero point one four lower at both settings.

Both paired confidence intervals lie below zero, supporting higher mean F1 for truncation in this evaluation.

The token budgets are comparable, but not identical.

Exact matches fall from fourteen for the uncompressed baseline to just one or two for LLMLingua, out of one hundred and fifty.

This measures exact agreement, not fluency.

[CHANGE TO SLIDE 12 — illustrative cases]

These examples show both failures and successes.

For the publication date, LLMLingua drops the year or says the date is absent.

For the electorate question, it returns Clutha-Southland instead of Wallace.

However, when evidence appears in the middle of a document, LLMLingua performs better than truncation.

These examples suggest possible information loss, but do not prove its cause.

[CHANGE TO SLIDE 13 — independent compression chunks]

Finally, Phi-2 has a context window of two thousand and forty-eight positions.

We therefore divide long documents into chunks of fourteen hundred measurement tokens and compress them independently.

This affects two hundred and ninety-six of three hundred outputs.

Each chunk receives the same compression rate, and coarse context filtering is disabled.

This is a resource-constrained adaptation, which motivates the whole-document variant discussed next.
